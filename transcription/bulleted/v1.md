v1 — first video (~11 min), earliest recording

- Benny explains VIX as the "fear index" — tracks regime changes, bull vs bear, captures market sentiment, and sentiment informs positions on the S&P.
- VIX chosen as the observation stream so the agent can learn whether it follows sentiment, with memory used to retain periods where there was incentive to act.
- Geraud asks whether VIX is a metric or a dataset. Benny: a dataset, an index.
- Benny does not know whether prior work has used VIX. Geraud puts a pin on it.
- Geraud explains why he will keep asking "what does prior work do": you want a dataset that is common and already researched in academia, unless you have a strong motivation otherwise — which for honours you usually want to avoid.
- His reasoning: the research question here is about a method, not about understanding a new dataset, so the dataset should be the common setting.
- Geraud's verdict: not a huge problem. The report must justify the dataset choice. Benny's sentiment/regime explanation is reasonable, not the strongest, but fine.
- Caveat: for publishable results, at least one dataset would have to be the standard one people use.
- Benny describes the data: ~9,180 days, 5-year train windows, 1-year test, walking forward by 1 year.
- Rationale for 5 years: don't train one model on the whole series, because financial data contains distinct regimes; research suggested regimes last ~5 years. Benny cannot recall the source but says he could back it up.
- Structure means no model trains on another model's training data and no two models test on the same data.
- Log returns used — standard in financial ML research, following Moody & Saffell.
- Geraud praises the "it follows from financial research" framing: you can cite the papers and let them do the justifying.
- Geraud's instruction: never say "I blindly followed" — not in the report, not when defending at the poster. Say it is the financial research standard, give one sentence on why it is the standard, and point to prior work for deeper reasons. One sentence is enough.
- Benny adds the VIX/S&P correlation is negative, which helps attribute results to the memory contribution rather than to a linear relationship between the two series.
- Geraud queries the arithmetic: what do 9,000 days, 24 years and 36 years each refer to? Benny: 24 years of overlap in total across the sliding windows.
- Geraud confirms the shape: X is a 5-year window, model is frozen after training, tested on the following year, repeat.
- Model output clarified: a position, from which returns follow, from which Sharpe is computed. Y is a single number.
- Geraud asks how many X inputs are in the training set. Benny: he had never thought of it that way — 31, one per window.
- Geraud's challenge: 31 X/Y pairs sounds extremely small for any machine learning training, independent of the data being financial. Asks for a note on it.
- His reasoning: a model needs many examples to learn which features to extract and how to use them for correct prediction, and the more complex the problem the more examples it needs. The underlying series being 9,180 days does not change that one window is one X.
